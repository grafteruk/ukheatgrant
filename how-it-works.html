How the Boiler Upgrade Scheme works

Plain-English guide · Reviewed July 2026 · England & Wales

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) is a UK Government grant that helps homeowners replace a fossil-fuel heating system with a low-carbon heat pump. It's administered by Ofgem and funded by the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero. This page explains, in plain terms, what you can get and how the money reaches you.

What you can get

The grant amounts are fixed by the scheme — they don't vary by installer or by how much you earn:

Heating system installedGrant
Air source heat pump (air-to-water)£7,500
Ground source or water source heat pump£7,500
Air-to-air heat pump (newly eligible in 2026; heats/cools but gives no hot water)£2,500
Biomass boiler (rural, off-gas-grid homes only)£5,000
New for off-gas oil & LPG homes — up to £9,000. From 21 July 2026 to 31 March 2027, homes off the gas grid that currently heat with oil or LPG can get an increased grant of £9,000 towards an air source or ground source heat pump — instead of the standard £7,500. It's a time-limited uplift, so the window matters if this is you.

You never touch the government's money

This is the part that surprises most people: the grant is paid directly to your installer, not to you. Your MCS-certified installer takes the grant amount off the price they quote, then claims it back from Ofgem afterwards. You simply pay the reduced figure. There are no government forms for you to fill in and nothing to claim back yourself.

The steps, start to finish

  • Survey & design. An MCS-certified installer assesses your home's heat loss and designs a suitable system.
  • Grant application. The installer applies to Ofgem on your behalf — approval typically takes a couple of weeks and can run alongside the design.
  • Install & commission. The system is fitted (usually 2–5 days), and the grant is deducted from your invoice. The install must be completed within 120 days of the grant application.

How long is it available?

The scheme is currently funded to run until 2030. Under the Government's Warm Homes Plan the Boiler Upgrade Scheme is backed by around £2.7 billion through to 2030, so there's no imminent cliff-edge. Funding is still released in annual budgets and a given year's pot can be used up before the year ends — it's not something to leave indefinitely, but you're not up against a hard deadline either. (The separate £9,000 oil/LPG uplift above is the one part with a fixed end date: 31 March 2027.)
