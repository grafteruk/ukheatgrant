What a heat pump really costs after the £7,500 grant

Updated July 2026 · UK Heat Pump Grant

This is the question that matters most, and the one that gets the most confusing answers online. Here's a plain breakdown of what a heat pump typically costs — and what you actually pay once the £7,500 grant is deducted.

 Typical rangeAfter £7,500 grant
Air source (small home)£8,000 – £11,000£500 – £3,500
Air source (average home)£11,000 – £14,000£3,500 – £6,500
Air source (large home)£14,000 – £18,000£6,500 – £10,500
Ground source£18,000 – £30,000£10,500 – £22,500

Illustrative examples only — typical market ranges to show the ballpark, not quotes, prices we set, or figures from named customers. Your actual price depends on your property and your installer's survey.

Off-gas oil or LPG home? Between 21 July 2026 and 31 March 2027 your grant may be £9,000 rather than £7,500 — making the "after grant" figures above roughly £1,500 lower. See who qualifies.

Why such a wide range? A heat pump is sized to your home's heat loss, which depends on insulation, number of rooms, ceiling heights and how warm you like things. A well-insulated 3-bed semi might need a smaller, cheaper system than a draughty 2-bed Victorian terrace. The only way to get a real price is a survey.

What's included in the price

  • The heat pump unit (the outdoor box)
  • A hot water cylinder (most homes need one)
  • Pipework, controls and electrical work
  • Installation labour
  • MCS certification (required for the grant)
  • A warranty — 5 years on most systems

What might cost extra

  • Larger radiators, if your current ones are too small
  • Underfloor heating (optional — not required)
  • Removal of your old boiler and tanks
  • Upgrading an old electrical consumer unit
Running costs: a properly-sized heat pump in a well-insulated home typically costs about the same to run as a modern gas boiler — sometimes a little more, sometimes a little less, depending on your electricity tariff and how your home performs. The big savings come from a heat-pump-friendly tariff (such as Octopus Cosy or Intelligent Go), which can cut running costs by 30–50%.
